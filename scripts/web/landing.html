The economics

Drinks carry the margin. The menu should carry the drinks.

18–24%
Industry-standard pour cost — roughly 80¢ of every beverage dollar comes back as gross margin, far ahead of food.
75–85%
Typical gross margin on spirits and cocktails. Wine runs 70–80%, draft beer 74–80%. Every extra pour is high-margin revenue.
+27%
Sales lift measured on menu items given descriptive labels in published menu-psychology research. Descriptions sell.

Sources: industry pour-cost and margin benchmarks (Restaurant365, 7shifts, Provi); Wansink, Painter & van Ittersum, descriptive-menu-label research, Cornell University / UIUC.

Most drink lists are a name and a price — the two facts least likely to sell a better bottle.

Beverages are the highest-margin thing a restaurant sells, and the menu is where nearly every drink decision happens. Yet the wine a guest has never heard of stays unordered, because nothing on the page says what it tastes like.

The research keeps finding the same thing: guests who can read tasting notes try unfamiliar pours and trade up to better bottles. Your servers can't stand at every table reciting grape varieties. The menu can — if it's more than a laminated list.

That's Unbottl: every bottle gets its region, grapes, and tasting notes; unfamiliar terms explain themselves with a tap; your staff picks sit where eyes land first. The guest orders like someone walked them through the list — because something did.
